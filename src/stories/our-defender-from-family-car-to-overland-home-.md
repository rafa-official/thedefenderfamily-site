When we bought our Defender in May 2018, it was already an expedition vehicle in spirit. It just didn’t know it yet.

It was a fairly standard Defender 110 Puma 2.4, with around 210,000 kilometers, seven seats, a tow ball, and slightly bigger wheels. Nothing fancy. Nothing extreme. Just a Defender with history, noise, charm, and the potential to become ours.

The idea was simple: travel a bit, see how we felt, and take it from there.

![Our First Day with our Land Rover Defender](/images/uploads/the-defender-family-first-day.jpg)

We did not want to build the ultimate overlanding machine before knowing if we even liked traveling that way as a family. So the first version was very basic. My Gallego friend Iago, who had self-converted a Mercedes Vito into a camper, helped me create Defender Overlander Version 1.0.

We removed the sixth and seventh seats, built a simple sleeping platform that worked when folding the rear seats, threw in some Decathlon camping gear, and called it a setup.

Was it perfect? No.

> "Did it work? Absolutely."

![First Trip in Camargue with our Land Rover Defender](/images/uploads/the-defender-family-overlander-1-01.jpg)
![First Trip in Camargue with our Land Rover Defender](/images/uploads/the-defender-family-overlander-1-02.jpg)
![First Trip in Camargue with our Land Rover Defender](/images/uploads/the-defender-family-overlander-1-03.jpg)

Our first real trip was to the Camargue area in the south of France, then back through Andorra. After that came a few weekend trips, small escapes, little tests. And slowly, the Defender started becoming part of the family.

![First Trip in Camargue with our Land Rover Defender](/images/uploads/land-rover-defender-river-crossing_001.jpg)

By 2019, we were ready for Version 2.0. For our longer summer trip, we bought a secondhand foldable Overcamp rooftop tent by Autohome. That changed everything. Suddenly, we had a little house on the roof. Comfortable, exciting, and a real upgrade from sleeping inside the car.

It was also a bit of a pain to open and close, especially the cover. Not terrible. Just enough to make you question your life choices every time you were tired, hungry, or it started raining.

That setup lasted a few years, with the tent mounted on Thule bars and some extra camping gear added along the way.

![Defender 110 with Autohome Rooftop Tent](/images/uploads/land-rover-defender-overlander_006.jpg)

![Family in their Land Rover Defender 110 with Autohome Rooftop Tent](/images/uploads/the-defender-family-corse-lake.jpg)

![Land Rover Defender 110 with Rooftop Tent at the Beach](/images/uploads/Overlanders-at-the-beach.jpg)

In 2020, I added a Bravo snorkel. After our summer trip through Corsica, France, and Sardinia, Italy, we realized that the inside needed a proper furniture system.

![Land Rover Defender 110 with Rooftop Tent at the Beach](/images/uploads/furniture-land-rover-defender-overlander-01.jpg)
![Land Rover Defender 110 with Rooftop Tent at the Beach](/images/uploads/furniture-land-rover-defender-overlander-02.jpg)
![Land Rover Defender 110 with Rooftop Tent at the Beach](/images/uploads/furniture-land-rover-defender-overlander-03.jpg)

That became a project.

But maybe the bigger question is: 

> “Why a Defender?”

For me, part of it goes back to growing up watching the Camel Trophy. That image of yellow Land Rovers crossing impossible places stayed somewhere in my brain forever (the picture of my brother on my bed with the Camel Trophy poster in the back, says it all). For Mariella, it was Africa. She had always been fascinated by those white UN Defenders roaming around remote places, looking like they were part of something bigger than just transportation.

{% image "/images/uploads/rafa-lanus-room-camel-trophy.jpg", "Rafa Room in the 80s with Camel Trophy Poster" %}

When I lived in Los Angeles, I could not afford a Defender. In the US, they only came in for a few years in the 90s, which made them ridiculously expensive. So I stayed loyal to the dream, and to the brand, with a white Discovery 2.

![Land Rover Defender 110 with Rooftop Tent at the Beach](/images/uploads/land-rover-discovery-2-in-death-valley.jpg)

I loved that car. But the V8 was thirsty. Very thirsty. I could almost hear the fuel leaving the tank and going directly into the engine. Sometimes I think it drank gas just because it was bored.

Anyway, coming back to our Defender.

In 2021, I had a big operation after they found a bad tumor in my kidney. Life gave me a second chance, and we decided to go all in.

Or what we thought was all in.

Two days after the operation, still from the hospital room, I called a local overlanding shop and started planning Defender Overlander Version 3.0. That is how I met Thomas, who today I can call a good friend.

We installed a Wild Land hardshell rooftop tent on a full-size Rhino-Rack platform. The decision was simple: after the Overcamp, we wanted something easier and faster. With the hardshell, you could open or close the tent in about a minute. In family overlanding, that is not a detail. That is quality of life.

![Land Rover Defender 110 with Rooftop Tent at the Beach](/images/uploads/defender-overlander-mother-daugher.jpg)

We also added a Rhino-Rack 270-degree awning with walls, giving us more shade, privacy, and living space. Then came the Engel fridge we still use, Front Runner chairs we still have, a bamboo table, Nomad Fox boxes, and a few other pieces of gear.

![Land Rover Defender 110 with Rooftop Tent at the Beach](/images/uploads/girl-sleeps-overland-defender.jpg)

At that point, things were getting serious.

That setup took us to southern Italy, Albania, Montenegro, Croatia, across Italy, and Sardinia. And honestly, it proved to be great.

![Land Rover Defender 110 with Rooftop Tent at the Beach](/images/uploads/land-rover-defender-overlander-riverbed.jpg)

![Land Rover Defender 110 with Rooftop Tent at the Beach](/images/uploads/Overlanders-at-the-beach-2.jpg)

> “The real deal..”

After our summer trip in the Transpirenaica in 2023, another chapter started. Thomas, the same friend who had helped us with the 2021 setup, was working with the Alu-Cab Iberia team and came to us with a collaboration proposal we couldn’t refuse.

Yes, very Godfather.

But instead of waking up with a horse head in the bed, we ended up with an Alu-Cab Icarus pop-top roof.

Much better deal.

Through that collaboration, the Defender became what it is today: an Alu-Cab Defender with the Icarus roof, an Alu-Cab 270 awning, and a shower cube. It was also the kind of partnership we value for The Defender Family: real gear, real travel, real use, and content created from experience, not from a parking lot.

![Land Rover Defender 110 with Rooftop Tent at the Beach](/images/uploads/land-rover-defender-alucab-desert-sunset.jpg)

Because the vehicle is registered in Spain, we also had to deal with the famous proyecto de homologación. That is a whole separate story. In simple terms, modifications need to go through an engineer, a lab, and finally ITV approval. It is expensive, confusing, and not exactly designed to protect your mental health.

![The Defender at sunset](/images/uploads/Alucab-defender-sunset.jpg)

So once you do it, you better do everything at the same time.

![The Defender at sunset](/images/uploads/land-rover-defender-overlander-alucab-camping-corse.jpg)

We lifted the Defender 10 centimeters, added Tough Dog shocks, Laser Lamps lights through another collaboration with their Spanish distributor, a D6 front bumper, differential protection, D6 rear corner protectors, and a Warn winch. D6 also became one of those brands we genuinely connected with, and we ended up doing some collaborations with them too. We may still add more, maybe rock sliders or side steps, because apparently this kind of project is never really finished.

![The Defender at sunset](/images/uploads/land-rover-defender-overlander-alucab.jpg)

But in the end, the Defender is not about the parts.

The tent, the awning, the fridge, the winch — they are tools. Useful tools, yes. Sometimes very expensive tools. But tools.

![The Defender at sunset](/images/uploads/the-defender-family-moments-in-scotland005.jpg)

The real point is traveling. Meeting people. Crossing borders. Getting stuck. Solving problems. Finding places you didn’t know existed. Sharing coffee with strangers. Being an adventurous family, not because everything goes perfectly, but because you keep going together when it doesn’t.

![The Defender at sunset](/images/uploads/overlanders-friends-land-rover-defender-alucab_010.jpg)

It is not the most comfortable vehicle. It is not the fastest. It is not the quietest. It is definitely not the cheapest to turn into an overlander.

But somehow, it became ours.

And maybe that is the whole thing.

One Life. Live It.
